1. Why special token ids need to be in config.json? (audio and image) and cannot be loaded from tokenizer?
    - The processor/tokenizer and model are independently loadable artifacts. The [Apertus 1.5 processor](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/processing_apertus1p5.py#L71-L89) reads the named token strings from the tokenizer and [builds the image/audio layout](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/processing_apertus1p5.py#L167-L175); the shared [`ProcessorMixin.__call__`](src/transformers/processing_utils.py#L643-L702) performs those text replacements before tokenization.
    - The model has no tokenizer reference, so it reads `image_token_id` and `audio_token_id` from `config.json` to [locate placeholder positions](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L793-L836), replace their embeddings, and group media during generation. This pattern is also used by [LLaVA](src/transformers/models/llava/modeling_llava.py#L190-L208) and [Gemma 3](src/transformers/models/gemma3/modeling_gemma3.py#L801-L819).
    - In the current model code, only the image/audio placeholder IDs are consumed; the other structural token strings are processor-side. Their IDs in the composite config provide an explicit artifact contract and are [cross-checked against the tokenizer during conversion verification](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/convert_apertus1p5_weights_to_hf.py#L267-L294).

1.5 Weights layout: is it standard to have lm head extra and not under model. ?
    - Yes. `model.*` is the backbone, while `lm_head.*` belongs to the outer task model; the keys mirror the Python module hierarchy. [Llama](src/transformers/models/llama/modeling_llama.py#L429-L441), [Mistral](src/transformers/models/mistral/modeling_mistral.py#L402-L414), and [Apertus](src/transformers/models/apertus/modeling_apertus.py#L423-L435) all use `self.model` plus a top-level `self.lm_head`.
    - The literal backbone prefix is architecture-specific rather than universal: for example, [GPT-2 uses `transformer.*` plus `lm_head.*`](src/transformers/models/gpt2/modeling_gpt2.py#L638-L648). Also, if embeddings and the head are tied, [`save_pretrained()` may omit the duplicate tied tensor](src/transformers/modeling_utils.py#L3588-L3590); Apertus 1.5's [pruned-head config forbids tying](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L140-L155), so its head is stored separately.

1.75 Is it standard to ship every weight under same key model. ? Or is it better to store as separate checkpoints?
    - No, not every tensor must literally start with `model.`; keys should follow the Python module tree. In [LLaVA](src/transformers/models/llava/modeling_llava.py#L130-L137) and [Gemma 3](src/transformers/models/gemma3/modeling_gemma3.py#L781-L789), subcomponents live inside the bare composite model, while their task wrappers put that model under `self.model` and keep `lm_head` outside ([LLaVA wrapper](src/transformers/models/llava/modeling_llava.py#L272-L279), [Gemma 3 wrapper](src/transformers/models/gemma3/modeling_gemma3.py#L936-L946)).
    - This hierarchy is the default PyTorch/HF serialization contract: [`save_pretrained()` starts from `model.state_dict()`](src/transformers/modeling_utils.py#L3556-L3559). Flattening or renaming keys is possible, but requires an explicit [`key_mapping`](src/transformers/modeling_utils.py#L4162-L4164), a registered conversion mapping, or custom load/save handling.
    - Logical names and physical files are independent. HF can [split one state dict into shards](src/transformers/modeling_utils.py#L3598-L3608) and [write a shared weight index](src/transformers/modeling_utils.py#L3684-L3701). Our [conversion script preserves separate source-named shards](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/convert_apertus1p5_weights_to_hf.py#L381-L398) while namespacing their keys under `model.language_model.*`, `model.vision_tokenizer.*`, and `model.audio_tokenizer.*` ([key remapping](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/convert_apertus1p5_weights_to_hf.py#L210-L225)); the resulting layout is visible in the [current composite weight index](../material/Apertus-1.5-8B-composite-hf/model.safetensors.index.json). Standalone component checkpoints are useful additional artifacts for independent reuse, not a replacement for the canonical composite.

2. why do need Vision Tokenizer and Audio tokenizer configs be in the config.json as well, cant they be in separate files? (Whats the benefit and drawback of this approach?)
    - Nested sub-configs are the standard composite pattern: [Apertus 1.5 declares all three in `sub_configs`](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L208-L250), as do [LLaVA](src/transformers/models/llava/configuration_llava.py#L49-L93) and [Gemma 3](src/transformers/models/gemma3/configuration_gemma3.py#L189-L222). `PreTrainedConfig.to_dict()` [recursively serializes config objects](src/transformers/configuration_utils.py#L1050-L1086), so one root `config.json` is self-contained and round-trips through ordinary `save_pretrained()` / `from_pretrained()`; the generic [composite-config test](tests/test_configuration_common.py#L94-L127) verifies this behavior. See the three nested sections in the [current composite config](../material/Apertus-1.5-8B-composite-hf/config.json).
    - On composite initialization, nested dictionaries are converted to typed config objects in [`Apertus1p5Config.__post_init__`](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L231-L250). [`Apertus1p5Model.__init__`](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L643-L654) then receives the composite config and constructs the language model, vision tokenizer, and audio tokenizer from their respective sub-configs; `from_pretrained()` subsequently loads the joint state dict.
    - Standalone classes receive only their component config. `Apertus1p5VisionTokenizerConfig` and `Apertus1p5TextConfig` declare [`vision_tokenizer_config`](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L89-L90) and [`text_config`](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L132-L133) as their `base_config_key`; generic config loading [extracts that nested section or finds a matching nested `model_type`](src/transformers/configuration_utils.py#L680-L689), which also covers WavTokenizer. Passing a config to the standalone constructor or `AutoModel.from_config()` constructs newly initialized weights; pretrained weights still have to be loaded.
    - Apertus 1.5's text-only model has an explicit, tested direct load from the composite: [integration test](tests/models/apertus1p5/test_modeling_apertus1p5.py#L818-L838) and [weight conversion mapping](src/transformers/conversion_mapping.py#L896-L897). No analogous direct-from-composite path is currently documented/tested for the vision and audio tokenizers; use their standalone checkpoints or load the composite and access its submodules.
    - Separate config files/subfolders are possible, but the composite would need custom coordination or external component references, weakening atomic offline loading. Separate standalone checkpoints remain useful additional artifacts, while the nested root config is the canonical composite configuration.

2.5 How do heterogeneous multimodal batches and media ordering work?
    - Processor level: different image sizes and arbitrary image/audio counts per sample—including zero—are supported. Nested inputs use one sub-list per text sample (empty sub-lists allowed) and validate ownership per sample; flat lists are also accepted and validate the total count. Flat media are consumed independently per modality in batch-sample order, then left-to-right placeholder order. Thus grouping is not required for execution, but nested input is safer because it catches misplaced media. See the [processor contract](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/processing_apertus1p5.py#L50-L63) and [uneven nested/flat tests](tests/models/apertus1p5/test_processing_apertus1p5.py#L132-L171).
    - Image processing may temporarily [group equal-shaped images and then restore their original order](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/image_processing_apertus1p5.py#L118-L142). The model crops every padded image to `image_sizes` and [encodes images one by one in row order](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L695-L716); it similarly slices and [encodes audio clips one by one in row order](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L743-L777).
    - Model level: image and audio have independent ordered streams, so arbitrary image/audio interleaving works. Features are concatenated in media-row order and [scattered into each modality's placeholder positions](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L883-L899), whose tensor traversal is batch-major. The model guarantees this mapping when rows obey the contract and checks total feature/token counts, but it cannot detect a same-sized swap between samples; callers bypassing the processor must preserve the order themselves. Beam expansion [reconstructs and repeats complete per-sample media groups without reordering them](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L1043-L1145).

2.75 How do standalone WavTokenizer and Apertus batch/pad audio?
    - Standalone WavTokenizer: the [feature extractor defaults to `padding=True`](src/transformers/models/wavtokenizer/feature_extraction_wavtokenizer.py#L121-L159). One clip remains at its own length; a ragged batch is zero-padded to its longest clip and receives a sample-level `padding_mask`. [`WavTokenizerModel.encode`](src/transformers/models/wavtokenizer/modular_wavtokenizer.py#L415-L454) encodes the complete rectangular batch at once. The mask is not applied inside the encoder; it only creates `audio_codes_mask` afterward. Therefore same-length batching matches individual encoding, but batch zero-padding can perturb valid end codes of shorter clips ([tests](tests/models/wavtokenizer/test_modeling_wavtokenizer.py#L241-L270)). `padding=False` is possible for standalone preprocessing, but a ragged result cannot be converted into one rectangular tensor and no padding mask is returned.
    - Apertus: the processor flattens all clips, normalizes them independently, and [pads them to one rectangular transport tensor plus a true-length mask](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/processing_apertus1p5.py#L179-L207). Apertus requires this mask, so `padding=False` is not supported there. The model then uses the mask to slice every row back to its true length and [calls WavTokenizer once per clip with batch size 1](src/transformers/models/apertus1p5/modular_apertus1p5.py#L743-L777). Thus no inter-example zero-padding reaches the codec, exactly analogous to cropping padded images before individual vision encoding.
    - “Without padding” here means without *batch/transport padding*. WavTokenizer still performs its own per-convolution boundary padding ([`EncodecConv1d`](src/transformers/models/encodec/modeling_encodec.py#L126-L171)), normally reflection padding, so arbitrary clip lengths produce `ceil(length / hop_length)` codes. This internal architectural padding is required and is independent of batching.
